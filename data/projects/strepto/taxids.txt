# Streptococcus 1301
#
# The clinical-bacteriology case of the FT paper, and the one of the two new projects that also has
# real read data (see data/fastq/strepto_lri_real.txt).
#
# Motivation is the framing of
#   Zhao Y, Zhang W, Zhang X (2024): "Application of metagenomic next-generation sequencing in the
#   diagnosis of infectious diseases." Front Cell Infect Microbiol 14:1458316.
#   https://doi.org/10.3389/fcimb.2024.1458316   (PMC11604630)
# a review of clinical mNGS by organ system. Streptococcus is the genus that appears in three of its
# sections at once -- bloodstream (4.1), CNS (4.2) and respiratory (4.3) infection -- and it is the
# genus where reporting the wrong member of it changes the clinical answer: S. pneumoniae is a
# pathogen to be treated, its mitis-group neighbours in the same sample are oral commensals.
#
# One genus only, as for the other projects of this paper.
#
# WHY THIS GENUS IS A CANDIDATE AT ALL, measured against data/common (RefSeq assembly summary of
# release 233 and the taxdump beside it, both as installed here):
#
#   RefSeq assemblies in the subtree      22,664       (tick-borne, for scale: 4,135)
#   sum of genome_size                    46.6 Gbp     (tick-borne: 5.8 Gbp)
#   species-rank taxa with >= 1 assembly     612
#   direct children of 1301 that carry genomes  148    -> the branching degree the refinement sees
#
# The reference taxonomy under Streptococcus is *almost* flat, and where it is not, it is flat in
# precisely the wrong places. NCBI has only four `species group' nodes under the genus:
#
#   Streptococcus dysgalactiae group  119603    S. equi, S. dysgalactiae                806 asm
#   Streptococcus anginosus group     671232    S. anginosus, S. intermedius,
#                                               S. constellatus                         397 asm
#   Streptococcus mitis group        3409772    S. mitis + 5 obscure species            253 asm
#   Streptococcus thalassemiae group 3409759    2 species                                 2 asm
#
# The "mitis group" node is the point. Biologically the mitis group is S. pneumoniae,
# S. pseudopneumoniae, S. mitis, S. oralis, S. infantis, S. sanguinis, S. gordonii,
# S. parasanguinis and relatives. In NCBI, all of those except S. mitis hang *directly* under the
# genus:
#
#   S. pneumoniae         1313   parent 1301 (genus)      9,263 asm
#   S. pseudopneumoniae 257758   parent 1301 (genus)        167 asm
#   S. oralis             1303   parent 1301 (genus)        259 asm
#   S. infantis          68892   parent 1301 (genus)         84 asm
#   S. sanguinis          1305   parent 1301 (genus)        153 asm
#   S. gordonii           1302   parent 1301 (genus)        106 asm
#   S. parasanguinis      1318   parent 1301 (genus)        177 asm
#   S. mitis             28037   parent 3409772 (group)     242 asm
#
# So every k-mer that S. pneumoniae shares with S. oralis or S. pseudopneumoniae has no common
# ancestor below the *genus*, and the LCA update strands it there. That is the headroom the
# refinement is supposed to claim, and it is unclaimed by construction rather than by accident --
# unlike Mycobacterium, where NCBI already supplies the MTBC (77643) and MAC (120793) nodes and the
# only thing left to refine is within-complex, i.e. the situation that killed the C. difficile study.
#
# Run bin/prescreen.sh streptococcus before building anything else; it decides whether that headroom
# really carries k-mers.
#
# THE TWO BRANCHES THAT CARRY NO NAMES are struck out below, and `refseq.updateScope=allButExcluded'
# in config.properties keeps them out of the LCA update as well -- without that line they would be
# gone from the database's content and still raise the k-mers of the species that stay.
#
# WHY. Under the LCA update a genome the taxonomy cannot place is not neutral but a sink. A k-mer
# shared between S. pneumoniae and an unnamed draft has its lowest common ancestor at the *genus*,
# because the draft hangs off a sibling branch of the entire mitis lineage; one such genome suffices
# to lift a species' k-mers clear out of it. The 468 genomes under `unclassified Streptococcus' hold
# 45.6 million k-mers between them, 109 of them more than 100,000 each, so they are whole genomes
# rather than fragments. The same is done in tick-borne, protozoa and parasites, and in protozoa it
# is measured: the refined root sp* rose from 0.804 to 0.889 and the two genera whose refinement had
# made sp* worse -- Leishmania and Trypanosoma -- both turned positive.
#
# WHAT IT DID NOT DO, measured before this project absorbed it (it was `streptonamed' until
# 2026-08-26): S. pneumoniae kept its 397 own k-mers exactly, while S. oralis went from 206,674 to
# 353,007, S. infantis rose by 136 per cent and S. pseudopneumoniae by 579. The unnamed branch was
# masking the pneumococcus's *relatives*, not the pneumococcus. What strands it is the mitis complex
# itself -- but that measurement was made under a contig cap that gave it two thirds of one assembly,
# which is why it is being repeated under maxGenomesPerTaxid.
#
# Nothing else is dropped. The genus has 147 children, of which 145 carry a name -- 140 species and
# five species groups -- and all of them stay.
