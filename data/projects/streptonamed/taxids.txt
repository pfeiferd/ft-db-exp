# The named children of Streptococcus (1301), and deliberately not the genus itself.
#
# WHY NOT 1301. Two of its 147 children carry no name of their own: 'unclassified Streptococcus'
# (2608887), with 468 species below it, and 'environmental samples' (83426). Between them they bring
# genomes into the database that the taxonomy cannot place, and under an LCA update an unplaced
# genome is not neutral -- it is a sink. A k-mer shared between S. pneumoniae and an unnamed draft
# has its lowest common ancestor at the genus, because the draft hangs off a sibling branch of the
# whole mitis lineage. One such genome is enough to lift a species' k-mers clear out of it.
#
# It is not a small effect here. In the database built from 1301, S. pneumoniae keeps 397 k-mers of
# its own where S. oralis keeps 206,674, and in 83 clinical samples not one classification ever
# landed below it. The 468 unnamed genomes hold 45.6 million k-mers between them, 109 of them more
# than 100,000 each, so they are whole genomes rather than fragments.
#
# Listing the 145 named children instead of the genus keeps every species the genus has and leaves
# only what has no name to lose.
